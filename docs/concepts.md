# Core concepts — simple language

## Volume
How many contracts changed hands. High volume means a lot of trading happened, but it does not automatically mean traders are bullish or bearish.

## Open interest
How many contracts remain open. It describes outstanding positions, not today's trading alone.

## Liquidity
How easily market participants can buy or sell without moving the price too much. Volume and open interest are useful activity indicators; bid/ask spread and order-book depth would provide a fuller view.

## Futures roll
Futures expire. Market participants who want to keep exposure close the expiring contract and move to a later contract. This often creates higher trading activity around March, June, September and December expiries.

## ADV
Average Daily Volume = monthly contracts / number of trading days. It makes months with different numbers of trading days easier to compare.

## Product-development lesson
A contract can be well designed but still struggle if clients do not need it or if market makers cannot support reliable liquidity. Product analysis therefore combines market statistics with questions about client need, execution quality and market-maker economics.
