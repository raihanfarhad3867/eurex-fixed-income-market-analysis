# Management memo — Fixed-income futures activity & product opportunity

## Executive message
The German government-bond futures complex is the activity anchor in this sample. Across Jan–Aug 2026, FGBS/FGBM/FGBL generated about **553.7 million contracts**, or roughly **3.28 million contracts per trading day** in aggregate. FGBL contributed about **39.3%**, FGBM **31.3%**, and FGBS **29.4%**.

March and June volumes were materially above the non-roll-month averages. The uplift was strongest in FGBS at about **118%**, versus roughly **61%** for FGBM and **59%** for FGBL. This should not be interpreted as roll activity alone: macro volatility and hedging demand also matter.

Among the selected 10-year sovereign futures, FGBL represented about **65.0%** of Jan–Aug volume versus about **19.3%** for FBTP and **15.7%** for FOAT.

## 4 September snapshot
- FGBS: 1,718,237 contracts traded; 2,643,073 previous-day OI
- FGBM: 1,891,812; 1,921,811 OI
- FGBL: 2,026,366; 2,106,126 OI

Daily-volume leadership and open-interest leadership are not the same: FGBL led daily volume among the three while FGBS had the largest previous-day OI.

## Product opportunity: FBEU as an adoption case
Euro-EU Bond Futures (FBEU) started trading in September 2025. The 4 Sep 2026 daily statistics show **13,291 contracts YTD** and **107 contracts of previous-day open interest**. That is very small relative to the established sovereign benchmarks. Eurex's 28 Aug 2026 amendment to the FBEU liquidity-provider program makes liquidity formation a relevant current product-management question.

## Recommendation
Do **not** jump from these data to "launch another contract." A stronger product-team question is: **what prevents adoption of FBEU, and which intervention would most improve executable liquidity?**

Priority next analyses:
1. Client interviews on real EU-bond hedge workflows
2. Market-maker economics and quote quality
3. Execution-cost comparison versus Bund/OAT/BTP proxy hedges
4. Cash-futures linkage and deliverable-basket analysis
5. Adoption funnel: active participants, block/TES usage, open-interest retention and expiry-to-expiry roll

The commercial decision should depend on whether FBEU solves a real hedging/execution problem and whether two-sided liquidity can become self-sustaining after incentives are reduced.
